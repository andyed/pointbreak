# The Point-Break Model

**One sentence:** model the Pleasure Point right-hander as a *zipper* — a breakpoint
traveling along a wave crest — generated by narrow-band swell meeting a planar shelf
tilted at an angle to the swell direction, with Middle Peak's A-frame as the symmetric
degenerate case and every other Santa Cruz spot a preset.

This document is the vehicle-independent parametrization. TouchDesigner and WebGL
implementations both derive from it; neither owns it.

## 1. Why a point break reduces to three relations

A peeling point wave is one of the few surf phenomena with a simple generating
function. The ingredients:

- **Narrow-band swell** — long-period groundswell from a distant storm. Energy is
  concentrated near one frequency and one direction (unlike local wind chop, which is
  broadband and directionally spread).
- **An angled planar shelf** — bathymetry that shallows gradually, with its dip
  direction rotated by angle θ from the swell direction. At Pleasure Point this is the
  gently sloping mudstone shelf; the tilt is the entire mechanism.

In Mead & Black's (2001a,b) bathymetric-component taxonomy this shelf is a **wedge**
— their name for exactly this: a planar component tilted against the favored swell
direction, the main breaking component of most world-class breaks. Our "sections" are
their **ridge**/**pinnacle** components (local bed steepenings that drop α and spike
intensity), and Middle Peak's bump is a **focus**. The reduction isn't a cartoon; it
keeps the two components that matter and drops the preconditioning ones (ramp,
platform) that only organize the swell before it arrives. USGS OFR 2007-1270
(Storlazzi et al.) surveyed the actual Pleasure Point bathymetry — the shelf-geometry
ground truth when we want real numbers for the tilt and slope.

Three standard relations turn those ingredients into a peeling wave:

### 1.1 Dispersion and shoaling

Deep-water dispersion: ω² = g·k. General (finite depth): ω² = g·k·tanh(k·h).
Phase speed c = ω/k → √(g·h) in shallow water.

As depth h decreases the wave slows, and conservation of energy flux makes it grow:
Green's law, H ∝ h^(−1/4) (shallow-water approximation — adequate here; we are not
doing coastal engineering). Slower + taller = steeper.

### 1.2 Refraction

Because c depends on h, wave crests obey Snell's law across depth contours:
sin(θ)/c = constant. Crests bend toward shallow water. On the tilted shelf, one end
of the crest is always in shallower water than the other — the crest sweeps around
the point, which is why Pleasure Point catches NW swell and folds it along the shelf.

### 1.3 Break criterion

The wave breaks where height exceeds a fraction of depth — McCowan's criterion,
H/h ≈ 0.78. On the tilted shelf this condition is met at one end of the crest first,
then progressively along it as the wave advances.

That progression **is the peel**. The breakpoint travels along the crest at

    V_p ≈ c / sin(α)

where α is the **peel angle** (Walker 1974: the angle between the advancing
whitewater front and the unbroken crest). α → 0 is a closeout (the whole wave breaks
at once, V_p → ∞); α → 90° is the slowest possible peel (V_p = c). Walker's makeable
criterion — a surfer with attainable speed V_s can make the wave iff V_s ≥ c/sin(α) —
puts the practical floor near α ≈ 30°; Hutt et al. (2001) calibrated the whole band
against surfer skill (beginners ≈ 70–90°, intermediates ≈ 46–55°, advanced ≈ 29–45°,
pros down to ≈ 20°). The shelf tilt θ sets α almost directly. One geometric parameter
spans the entire local taxonomy, consistent with those verified bands:

| α (peel angle) | character | Santa Cruz reference |
|---|---|---|
| ~30–40° | fast, sectiony, barreling | The Slot (Steamer Lane) |
| ~45–55° | fast but makeable, hollow | First Peak, The Hook |
| ~55–65° | "soft walls that peel wonderfully" | Second Peak / Jack's |
| ~65–75° | slow, mellow, endless | Cowell's |

### 1.4 Breaker type (the barrel knob)

The Iribarren number (surf similarity parameter) classifies how the wave breaks:

    ξ = tan(β) / √(H/L₀)

where β is bottom slope and L₀ deep-water wavelength. Battjes (1974) thresholds
(deep-water form): spilling ξ₀ < 0.5, plunging 0.5–3.3, surging > 3.3. Visually this
is a single "barrel-ness" parameter controlling lip geometry and foam behavior. It is
independent of peel angle — a wave can peel slowly AND plunge — which is why two
knobs, not one, are needed for spot character.

Refinement for later: Mead & Black (2001c) show ξ under-resolves differences *within*
the plunging class; their orthogonal-seabed-gradient → vortex-ratio regression
(Y = 0.065·X + 0.821, R² 0.71) is the better dial for barrel *shape* once plunging.
v0 uses ξ alone; the vortex-ratio refinement waits for the substrate phase.

## 2. The zipper: kinematic runtime reduction

Bathymetry, refraction, and the break criterion are the *justification*. At runtime
none of them need to be computed. The kinematic reduction:

- **Crest** — a curve advancing shoreward at phase speed c(t) (slowing as it shoals).
- **Zipper phase** s_b(t) — a break-point position traveling along the crest at V_p.
  This is the model's single moving degree of freedom.
- **Ahead of the zipper** (s > s_b): unbroken green face, steepening. In Gerstner
  terms: crest sharpness Q ramping toward its cusp limit as the wave "feels bottom."
- **At the zipper**: the active pocket — maximum steepness, the lip, the visual locus
  of intensity. Iribarren ξ decides its geometry (spilling fringe vs thrown lip).
- **Behind the zipper** (s < s_b): whitewater — bright foam with exponential decay,
  τ ≈ 3–8 s, advected slightly shoreward.

**Sections**: noise on the shelf depth → local spots meet the break criterion early →
the wave breaks ahead of the zipper. Implemented as a 1-D noise field along the crest;
where it exceeds a threshold, a secondary zipper fires. Section density is the
"sectiony-ness" knob. A section the main zipper cannot outrun is a closeout.

**Sets**: waves arrive in groups because nearby frequencies beat. Two spectral
components at f and f+Δf give a group envelope with period 1/Δf — free grouping from
a two-component swell. Δf ≈ 0.004–0.01 Hz yields set cycles of ~100–250 s with
~4–8 waves per set, matching observed rhythm. (Group velocity = ½ phase velocity in
deep water — sets crawl relative to the waves in them, a real and visible effect.)

This is a 1-D phase traveling along a 2-D curve with a before/after material change.
A few uniforms; no fluid solver; no FFT required for the break layer itself. The
zipper motion — not water shading — is the recognizable signature of "a wave peeling."

## 3. The Pleasure Point model card

Canonical parameter values (the reference instance):

| parameter | symbol | value | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| swell direction | θ_s | 290–300° (NW) | winter/fall groundswell |
| peak period | T | 12–15 s | narrow-band |
| deep-water height | H₀ | 1–2.5 m | head-high days |
| spectral bandwidth | Δf | ~0.006 Hz | sets of ~5–7 |
| shelf slope | s | ~1:50 | gentle mudstone |
| shelf tilt vs swell | θ | 30–45° | the master knob |
| peel angle | α | 55–65° | derived; "peels wonderfully" |
| along-shelf gradient | dα/ds | slight decrease | wave speeds up down the line (toward First Peak character) |
| section noise | σ_h | low | occasional fast sections, rare closeouts |
| Iribarren | ξ | ~0.5–0.8 | plunging-ish but forgiving |
| ride duration | — | 30–60 s | zipper travel time; a musical-phrase timescale |
| foam decay | τ | ~5 s | |

## 4. Presets: the rest of town for free

Same machinery, different knob positions:

- **Cowell's** — α high (~70°), H low, ξ low (spilling), σ_h ≈ 0. Marshmallow.
- **Jack's / Second Peak** — the model card above.
- **First Peak / The Hook** — α ~50°, ξ up, slight σ_h. Faster takeoff.
- **The Slot (Steamer Lane)** — α ~35°, ξ high (plunging), σ_h high. Fast, sectiony,
  loves to barrel. Character comes from abruptness we *simulate as parameter extremes*
  rather than modeling the actual rock.
- **Middle Peak (the A-frame event)** — replace the tilted plane with a **localized
  shallow bump**. Break criterion is met first at the apex; two zippers fire and
  propagate outward in mirror symmetry. One extra parameter (bump width), and the
  only preset that is an *event* rather than a state. Bilaterally symmetric —
  kaleidoscope-native.

## 5. Deliberately out of scope

- **Barrel interior / tube ride POV** — a camera-and-geometry problem, not a model
  problem. Revisit only if the flat zipper earns it.
- **True shallow-water solver** (Boussinesq etc.) — coastal-engineering fidelity
  is not the goal; the zipper is a *reading* of the wave, not a hindcast.
- **Surfers** — the wave is the protagonist.
- **Swash/backwash, currents, kelp** — texture, not structure. Later, maybe.

## 6. Hooks

- **Live data ("today's ocean")** — CDIP directional spectra near Monterey Bay can
  supply T, H₀, θ_s, Δf in real time, so the model peels at the same period and
  heaviness as the wave outside. See `research/CDIP_LIVE_DATA.md`.
- **Musical mapping** (for the eventual Psychodeli port) — the ride is a *phrase*
  (moving locus of intensity), a barrel section is a sustained intense passage, a
  closeout is a phrase-ending drop, set/lull structure is song-section structure,
  and the Middle Peak A-frame is a downbeat event firing two symmetric zippers.
  Groundswell vs. wind chop = sustained structure (bass/harmony) vs. transient
  texture (hats/noise) — spectrum bandwidth as the expressive axis.
- **Substrate** — the zipper layer composites over any water surface: an FFT
  deep-water ocean (TD: the community FFTOcean asset; web: Tessendorf-style IFFT or
  a Gerstner sum). The break layer is the novel contribution; the substrate is
  commodity.
