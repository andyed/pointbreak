## Corrections to the audit — read these before quoting it

### 1. The tide response is NOT inverted (audit item 5, second bullet) — REFUTED

Tide sign is correct everywhere in the depth path. The break line, the breaking
gate, and the broken **area** all respond with correct polarity to a dropping
tide: `brk_mean` over the wet transect at The Hook is 0.460 at tide −0.8 m vs
0.238 at +0.7 m (1.93×), and 4.95× over the surf-zone band z = −170…−60 m.

The real defect is narrower: **`brk` reaches the surface only as a
subtraction** (`decay = 1.0 - 0.68*brk`), the whitewater generator has no
dependence on how wide the broken zone is, and the one term that does scale
with area sits below the renderer's foam visibility gate. So a dropping tide
breaks over 1.9–5× more area and renders ~2.5× **fewer** above-threshold foam
pixels, on top of a water body that brightens ~20 sRGB values (Beer–Lambert
bottom return) and collapses foam contrast.

**And the audit's frame should never have been graded**: `sim=42` is a lull at
*both* tides (L≥205 = 3 px vs 19 px). The audit's own provenance caveat — one
frozen clock — is exactly what bit it.

### 2. The ~200° shore normal is REFUTED, and the incidence story inverts

Measured seaward normals are **126–151°** at SC116 and 136–150° at the canon
spots; `N_ref = 146° ± 6` at the 15 m contour. So incidence at the reef is
**+42…+70° off normal** (median D_p 194° → +48°), not the audit's −12…+21°.
Also: SC116 sits **1048 m** offshore in −14.68 m NAVD88, not the "~150 m" the
research doc claims.

Consequence: the deep-water angle is **not recoverable** at Pleasure Point, so
the model's direction input should be the *nearshore observation*, not a
deep-water angle refracted in. That is now written into MODEL.md §2.6.

### 3. The headland premise is WRONG on both halves — and the fix is ~10 lines

The rendered coastline is **100% DEM-derived** (`coastCurve` contributes
nothing to the visible land silhouette in web-three), and Sewer Peak's shipped
DEM patch **already contains 111.5° of coastline rotation**, including the OSM
apex at u=0. The headland is in the data *and already in the shipped drone
frame* — it just occupies the bottom 16% of frame height (land pixels start at
row 605/720) because the drone camera's cross-shore footprint stops at z=+173 m
while the corner's limbs sweep to z=+320 m.

**This is a camera-composition defect, not a geometry defect.** Minimal fix: one
new camera preset in `CAM_PRESETS` (main.js:300–308), e.g.
`{ name: 'Point', pos: () => [0, 560, 200], target: () => [0, 0, 140] }`.
Track 1b's "build the real shoreline from data/osm" is therefore **not needed**.

### 4. The deployed essay was NOT generated by the indicted config — worry withdrawn

The live `/pleasurepoint/` sim is the 2026-08-10 14:57 build with
`m4Enabled = false` — the authored-break-line era, pre-M4-default, pre-M5,
pre-nose. The audit's concern that published figures rest on the noise-owned
peel does not apply to what is deployed. (Counterfactual measured: dry-land
pixels 22.4% → 51.6% under the current build's hashes, mean|diff| 7.07% of full
range; the current build reproduces the repo's own corrected figures at 1.04%.)

### The seam and the "light shaft" are ONE thing — the reef bar, not the DEM

Both artifacts trace to the synthetic M5 wedge (`makeReefFn`, `bed.js:150–197`)
being an **unbounded straight bar** that runs 200–300 m offshore into 6–7 m of
water. The "light-shaft wedge" *is* that bar, rendered by the Beer–Lambert
bottom-visibility term (`shaders.js:517–521`) — not a light effect at all, but
the seabed showing through. The vertical seam is the M4 break-line bake
snapping **110–180 m between two adjacent 4.72 m bake texels** where that
offshore bar crosses the depth-limited criterion, then painted by `shoreT`
(`shaders.js:498`) and driving the isolated whitecap column via
`d = breakLine(x) - z`.

Per-spot single-texel jumps: Sewers 179.5 m, 38th 155.6, Sharks 148.6, The Hook
145.8, First Peak 109.8, Second Peak 46.2. **On the raw measured DEM the same
bake is smooth to 2.2 m** (plane bed: 0.1 m), so the audit's DEM-noise
attribution is wrong *for this artifact* — the reef bar causes it. Ψ is
confirmed irrelevant, as the audit suspected.

Fix direction: bound the wedge cross-shore (switch the reef off where the
natural bed is deeper than the wedge), and slew-limit the bake (a 2.0 m z per
m x limit brings the jumps to 9.4–9.5 m).
