# Domain ballot — Surf seat, jury 2 (2026-09-24)

Judged against CURL_TRUTH §4 and the three field sheets (Second Peak, 2026-08-15,
events A/B/C). 45 frames, `<rig>_<arm>_<clock>.jpg`, all opened. What is drawn,
not what the flags intend.

Reading the frames first, one fact shapes everything below: at both Second Peak
cliff rigs the five arms collapse into two visual classes. `default`,
`tubeclassic` and `sections` are indistinguishable frame for frame at 52/54/56 s;
`bore` and `all` are indistinguishable from each other. Only at Sewers close do
all five differ. Image-plane face heights used for ratios: Second Peak set wave
H_f ≈ 50 px (crest y≈295, foot y≈345), Surfline forcing H_f ≈ 40 px, Sewers close
H_f not measurable (camera sits on the face; knuckle size used instead).

Convention: PASS / FAIL / NA, one clause of evidence, frame and clock named.
"Borderline" is recorded as PASS or FAIL with the ratio, so it can be re-judged.

## (1) Ranking at the Second Peak cliff rigs — "reads as Pleasure Point breaking" (the site test)

1. **bore** — the only class that draws a knuckle nose and a thick round-topped bore level with the crest (`setwave_bore_52`), peeling the right way, no cavity.
2. **all** — tied with bore on every frame; ranked second only because the extra flags it carries (ribbon, sections, plume) produce nothing visible here, so it is bore with more machinery.
3. **sections** — tied with default; the flag does nothing visible at these clocks, and doing nothing at Second Peak is at least not wrong.
4. **default** — dark face, small hook, but no head: a thin ridge then a patchy white band (`setwave_default_52`). Fails the "not a band" must-not.
5. **tubeclassic** — tied with default in pixels; last because it is the arm whose purpose is the one thing §3 says must never appear at Second Peak. It does not fire here, which is correct, but it cannot rank above the arms that add nothing by design.

Note for the record: 3/4/5 are a three-way visual tie; 1/2 are a two-way tie. Nothing in the 18 Second Peak frames separates them.

## (2) Ranking at Sewers close — "reads as a breaking wave at all"

1. **all** — a lumpy compact knuckle with white pouring down the face under it (`sewers_close_all_52`), then a smooth spilling sheet at 54. The only arm whose head looks like water at both clocks.
2. **sections** — a compact knuckle at 52 (`sewers_close_sections_52`), but sharp, a bright wedge with a small spike; smooth sheet at 54.
3. **tubeclassic** — at 54 the best head of the rig (rounded lumpy foam mass, `sewers_close_tubeclassic_54`), but at 52 it hangs a translucent fin the full visible face with a hole behind it.
4. **bore** — the slab, slightly smoother than default (`sewers_close_bore_52`), shards at 54.
5. **default** — a faceted white kite lying on the crest (`sewers_close_default_52`) that breaks into shards (`_54`). Not water.

## (3) The single defect a surfer would call out first, per arm

- **default**: "Nothing's breaking — that's a feather line along the whole wave with no head" (Second Peak), and at Sewers "there's a sheet of ice on the lip" (`sewers_close_default_52`).
- **bore**: "The head's a smooth white sausage — no lumps, no lip flicking off it, and there's a second hump lifting behind it" (`setwave_bore_56` x 540–720). At Sewers the bore paints behind a head that is still the ice slab.
- **tubeclassic**: "That's a sail, not a lip" — a glassy translucent fin down the whole face with a gap behind it at a spot that does not barrel on this day (`sewers_close_tubeclassic_52`); and at Second Peak the flag changes nothing.
- **sections**: "The head is a bright spike" (`sewers_close_sections_52`), and at Second Peak the flag changes nothing at these clocks; no wall-curtain-plume event is drawn anywhere in the matrix.
- **all**: "Smooth white cap, no lip line, and the whitewater behind it is one flat tone" (`sewers_close_all_54`, `setwave_all_52`); the impact plume never shows.

## (4) What `all` still lacks against the footage, in order of what a viewer notices first

First, the whitewater is the wrong material everywhere: the footage's broken water is matte, opaque, lumpy white that ages into a broad soft foam field, while `all` renders it as a flat white plane with rain-like streak sprites at Sewers and as horizontally blurred streaks with leopard patches and specular glitter at Second Peak, so the eye reads motion blur and sparkle rather than foam. Second, the head is a smooth rounded white cap with no thin, scalloped, short-lived hook flicking off its crest and no crest lumps on the bore behind it, so it lacks the one detail (sheet A f1050–f1070, sheet C f870–f875) that makes a Pleasure Point knuckle look alive rather than extruded. Third, the frame has no single subject: three parallel lines of comparable white weight plus foreground glitter, and at 54–56 s a second white-capped hump rising behind the head, where the footage shows one live head over a quiet grey sea with everything inshore subordinate.
